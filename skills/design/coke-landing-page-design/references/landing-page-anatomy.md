# Landing Page Anatomy

Use this as a decision aid, not a mandatory section list. A focused landing page should include only the elements that support the audience, traffic source, offer, and conversion goal. Always account for value proposition, CTA, proof, friction, and trust.

## Core Elements

1. **URL and search intent**
   - Use a descriptive, memorable URL when the project controls routing.
   - Align page title, meta description, H1, and above-the-fold copy with the user's search or ad intent.

2. **Brand signal**
   - Show the logo or product identity clearly.
   - Keep visual language consistent with the ad, email, product, or brand touchpoint that sent the user there.

3. **Above-the-fold promise**
   - Make the first viewport answer: who is this for, what is offered, why it matters, and what to do next.
   - Avoid cramming every argument into the hero. The first viewport should create enough clarity and confidence to continue.

4. **USP and headline**
   - State the unique selling proposition in customer language.
   - Prefer specificity over cleverness. Include the main benefit, pain point, or transformation.

5. **Offer**
   - Make the offer easy to understand: what the user gets, what it costs or requires, why it is valuable, and what happens after action.
   - Express both features and benefits when the category needs detail.

6. **Primary CTA**
   - Use one dominant action path. Repeat it where momentum naturally peaks.
   - CTA copy should describe the action and outcome, not just "Submit" or "Learn more."

7. **Visual media**
   - Use visuals that explain the offer: product screenshots, workflow examples, before/after views, demo clips, customer context, or authentic photography.
   - Avoid generic stock imagery, fake UI, or decoration that does not clarify value.

8. **Benefits and features**
   - Lead with benefits, then support with features.
   - Use 3-6 core points for simple offers. Use grouped feature sets for complex products.

9. **Proof**
   - Include social proof when available: testimonials, reviews, customer logos, case studies, usage numbers, awards, certifications, or press.
   - Place proof near the claim it supports. Do not invent or exaggerate proof.

10. **Objection handling and FAQ**
   - Address the doubts that block action: price, setup, trust, effort, timeline, cancellation, security, privacy, compatibility, and support.
   - Use FAQ only for real objections or decision questions, not filler.

11. **Final CTA**
   - Give users a final conversion point after they have seen enough proof.
   - Restate the offer, reduce risk, and keep the next action clear.

12. **Footer, contact, and legal**
   - Include credibility basics: company identity, contact route, privacy, terms, and other legally or commercially expected links.
   - On focused campaign pages, keep the footer useful but not distracting.

## Page Flow Patterns

- **Short lead capture:** hero, offer details, proof, FAQ or risk reversal, final CTA.
- **SaaS trial:** hero, product visual, benefits, workflow, integrations, proof, pricing or plan teaser, FAQ, final CTA.
- **High-ticket demo:** hero, pain or opportunity, proof, process, outcomes, objections, qualification CTA, trust/legal footer.
- **Waitlist or launch:** hero, vision, who it is for, early proof or founder credibility, what happens after joining, FAQ, final CTA.
- **Paid search page:** tight message match, minimal navigation, offer clarity, CTA, proof, risk reversal, fast load.

## Design Notes

- Keep a single conversion focus. Remove navigation or secondary links when they distract from the campaign goal.
- Use visual hierarchy to prioritize the CTA, promise, and proof. Use size, contrast, spacing, and typography intentionally.
- Make brand consistency visible through typography, color, image style, tone, and interaction style.
- Keep performance and mobile layout part of anatomy. A beautiful page that loads slowly or breaks on mobile is not conversion-ready.
