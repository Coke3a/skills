# Conversion Frameworks

Use one primary framework to create a clear persuasion path. Use a second pattern only when it solves a specific weakness, such as missing proof or unresolved risk.

## Choose The Framework

- Use **PRESTO** when the page needs a full persuasive journey from pain to offer while staying clear and structured.
- Use **AIDA** when the visitor is early in awareness and needs attention, interest, desire, then action.
- Use **PAS** when the audience already feels a painful problem and needs the page to make the cost of inaction explicit.
- Use **BAB** when transformation is the main sale and the page must contrast current state with a better future.
- Use **4P** when skepticism is the barrier: problem, promise, proof, proposal.
- Use **Fear-Logic-Action** when inaction has concrete consequences and the buyer also needs rational justification.
- Use **Social Proof Sandwich** when trust is weak or the category is crowded.
- Use **Objection Crusher** when buyers hesitate over price, complexity, switching cost, risk, or credibility.
- Use **Risk Reversal** when the offer asks for money, time, reputation, or operational change.
- Use **Contrast Close** when the status quo is expensive and measurable.
- Use **Transformation Timeline** when the outcome feels abstract, long, or uncertain.
- Use **Personal Identity Alignment** when the buyer's self-image, role, or aspiration is central to the decision.

## Framework Notes

### PRESTO

Structure: Pain, Resonate, Educate, Simplify, Testify, Offer.

Use it to build an end-to-end page that starts with the visitor's struggle, shows understanding, teaches a useful insight, simplifies the offer, supports it with proof, and presents a clear next step.

### AIDA

Structure: Attention, Interest, Desire, Action.

Use it for launch pages, paid traffic pages, product introductions, and simple lead-generation pages. Keep the hero sharp, make benefits concrete, build desire with visuals and proof, and make the CTA unmissable.

### PAS

Structure: Problem, Agitate, Solution.

Use it for urgent pain points, operational failures, wasted time, lost revenue, compliance risk, or recurring frustration. Agitate with real consequences, not fearmongering. Resolve quickly with a credible solution.

### BAB

Structure: Before, After, Bridge.

Use it when the product creates a visible transformation. Show the current painful state, make the better future tangible, then explain the product as the bridge.

### 4P

Structure: Problem, Promise, Proof, Proposal.

Use it when the page needs a logical path and stronger evidence. The promise should be specific, proof should be placed close to claims, and the proposal should make the next step feel low-friction.

### Fear-Logic-Action

Structure: Fear, Logic, Action.

Use it when delay creates a measurable downside. Pair emotional urgency with rational evidence such as ROI, saved time, reduced risk, or lower operational cost.

### Social Proof Sandwich

Structure: broad credibility, core offer, detailed proof.

Use it when trust needs reinforcement throughout the page. Place logos, metrics, testimonials, case notes, reviews, and certifications near the claims they support.

### Objection Crusher

Structure: identify objections, answer them throughout the page, reinforce with specifics.

Use it when likely objections are known. Common objections include price, setup effort, migration, security, support, reliability, cancellation, and whether the product fits the user's exact situation.

### Risk Reversal

Structure: make the user's perceived risk explicit, then reduce or transfer it.

Use it for paid plans, subscriptions, demos that require commitment, high-ticket services, or enterprise workflows. Examples include free trials, cancellation clarity, guarantees, proof of support, onboarding promises, or "no credit card" messaging.

### Contrast Close

Structure: compare doing nothing with taking action.

Use it when the cost of inaction can be shown with time, money, missed opportunities, manual effort, errors, churn, downtime, or stress.

### Transformation Timeline

Structure: starting point, milestones, expected change, proof or next step.

Use it when the user worries that the outcome is hard, slow, or uncertain. Make the journey feel concrete with steps, timeframes, milestones, screenshots, or examples.

### Personal Identity Alignment

Structure: mirror the buyer's self-image, show the future identity, offer the product as the behavior of that identity.

Use it when the product helps users feel like a better operator, founder, parent, designer, engineer, creator, leader, or expert. Avoid manipulative identity pressure.
